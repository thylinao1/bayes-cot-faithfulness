Interactive model of direct and indirect effects

A language model can write a chain of thought, a sequence of reasoning steps, before it gives its final answer. The project asks how much of a prompt's effect on that answer passes through the chain of thought.

That part is the indirect effect (NIE), and the part that bypasses the chain of thought is the direct effect (NDE). This page shows a simplified version of the project's model, with one slider for each arrow in the diagram.

Parts of the display

Each card describes one part of the display and names the formal quantity behind it.

Indirect path

Dots on the upper path move from the prompt through the chain of thought to the answer. The line width and the number of dots grow with the indirect effect (NIE).

prompt → chain of thought → answer

Direct path

Dots on the lower path move from the prompt straight to the answer. This path grows with the direct effect (NDE), the part of the effect that the chain of thought does not carry.

prompt → answer, bypassing the chain of thought

Share of the effect through the chain of thought

The percentage is the indirect effect divided by the total effect (TE), where TE = NDE + NIE. The project calls this share faithfulness.

NIE / TE · methodology page

Uncertainty and sensitivity in the project's method

The project's method puts an uncertainty range on this share, called a credible interval. A credible interval is the range of values that the Bayesian model considers plausible. The calibration of these intervals is tested separately and reported.

The method also reports how much hidden confounding would overturn the result. Hidden confounding is an unmeasured influence on both the chain of thought and the answer.

The summary for it is rho*, the value of rho at which the indirect effect crosses zero. rho is the residual correlation between the chain-of-thought measurement and the answer. Residual means the part of each that the prompt does not explain.

rho* is computed from a maximum-likelihood fit. It does not come from a posterior, and the project's implementation does not check optimizer convergence.

rho* answers only this question about confounding. It does not change when the direct path grows stronger, and it cannot rank models.
