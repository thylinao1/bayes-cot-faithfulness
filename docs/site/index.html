01 · the problem

Frontier safety leans on reading the model's reasoning, but that reasoning might be fake.

◎

Scalable oversight breaks

Plans for overseeing superhuman models assume the written reasoning drives the answer. If CoT is decorative, the oversight signal is fake, and current measurements cannot tell you how fake it is.

◎

Deceptive alignment slips through

A deceptive model can write innocent-looking CoT while the actual computation does something else. Telling that apart from "the CoT genuinely drives the answer" needs a causal (not correlational) analysis, along with proper power calculations.

◎

Evaluations stop being credible

Faithfulness has become load-bearing in AI-control evals. Point estimates without credible intervals cannot tell you whether model A is actually more faithful than model B, or whether the difference is sampling noise.

"We measured the faithfulness of CoT reasoning, but we didn't measure how confident we should be in that number. That is the gap."

02 · the approach

Treat CoT as a causal mediator and estimate it with hierarchical Bayes.

Import 25 years of causal mediation analysis from epidemiology and econometrics into LLM interpretability. The prompt's effect on the answer decomposes into two paths:

  • Natural direct effect (NDE), which is the path that bypasses CoT. A high NDE / TE ratio means the reasoning is decorative.
  • Natural indirect effect (NIE), which is the path that flows through CoT. A high NIE / TE ratio means the reasoning is causally faithful.

Estimation is hierarchical Bayesian in PyMC. The output is a full posterior distribution rather than a point estimate, with calibrated credible intervals that can be used to make deployment decisions.

Why Bayesian: uncertainty that can be checked rather than assumed. Calibration is a property of the model, the priors, the implementation and the target, not of the framework, so we demonstrate it separately with computation checks and repeated-sampling performance, and we report where it fails. Hierarchical pooling across prompts and seeds, and posterior model comparison (Bayes factors, PSIS-LOO), follow the same estimator.

mediation.py 
import pymc as pm

with pm.Model() as _model:
    alpha   = pm.Normal("alpha", mu=0., sigma=1.5)
    beta    = pm.Normal("beta",  mu=0., sigma=2.0)
    gamma   = pm.Normal("gamma", mu=0., sigma=1.5)
    sigma_m = pm.HalfNormal("sigma_m", sigma=1.0)

    # mediator equation:  M | X
    pm.Normal("M_obs", mu=gamma * X,
              sigma=sigma_m, observed=M)

    # outcome equation:  Y | X, M
    pm.Bernoulli("Y_obs",
        logit_p=alpha * X + beta * M, observed=Y)

    trace = pm.sample(1500, tune=1500, chains=4,
                      target_accept=0.95)
03 · the hard assumption

Every faithfulness number rests on an assumption you cannot check. We turn it into a dial.

Causal mediation needs one thing it can never verify from data: that nothing unmeasured sits between the CoT and the answer once the prompt is fixed. In a language model that is almost never true, because the CoT and the answer read from the same hidden activations. Instead of assuming the problem away, we add a single sensitivity knob, ρ, and watch what the faithfulness verdict does as we turn it.

A naive audit can over-trust the reasoning

Prior causal-CoT work sets ρ to zero and stops. That is the black vertical line in the middle of the plot. When the real confounding is positive, the natural case for a model whose CoT and answer share hidden state, sitting at ρ = 0 reports a faithful path that is too high. The gap does not shrink with more data, because it is confounding and not noise. The sweep makes that gap visible and bounded instead of invisible. The frontier itself, ρ*, is computed from a maximum-likelihood fit, not from a posterior, and the current implementation returns the optimizer's parameters without checking convergence. Read it as a point-estimate sensitivity curve; the uncertainty-aware replacement is a separate quantity, labelled as such wherever both appear.

One number per model is the wrong unit

Faithfulness is not a single property of a model. It varies by prompt and by task, and some prompts have far fewer usable traces than others. The hierarchical model gives each prompt its own faithfulness slope drawn from a shared population, so a prompt with five traces borrows strength from the rest instead of swinging wildly on its own. The output is a population slope with its posterior interval, plus a direct read on how much prompts disagree. The interval's calibration is tested, not assumed, and the test is reported.

04 · does it catch anything?

The auditor is tested against unfaithfulness we planted ourselves.

Before reporting any verdict, the auditor is run against a case we constructed: an authoritative but wrong hint on a question the model can answer. The auditor must flag silent hint-following, clear genuine reasoning, and clear the model that follows a hint while saying so, which is honest deference, not deception.

unit test on synthetic data

On synthetic worlds with known mechanisms (synthetic), the estimator separates a faithful CoT (faithful path +0.40, overturned only at ρ* = 0.69) from a decorative one (+0.02, overturned at ρ* = 0.05). ρ* is the mediation-analysis cousin of VanderWeele's E-value: how strong a residual mediator-outcome correlation would have to be before this particular conclusion flips. It answers that one question and no other. In particular it does not move when a direct bypass grows: with the mediator coefficient fixed, the crossing stays at 0.624695 while the mediated share falls from 100 % to 1.6 %. Read it beside the direct, indirect and total effects, never instead of them.

real open-model runs, pilot

On real open-model runs (pilot), a planted wrong hint was followed on every run whose one-event detection rate cleared the 10 % target, at 12.5 % (n = 16), 13.6 % (n = 22), and 36.9 % on the largest run (n = 103). Most of that following was silent by the heuristic auditor labels (human labeling in progress): the chain-of-thought did not disclose the hint, which the auditor flagged. Read that silent share as a first look, not a final measurement.

What this is not

Not yet a validated benchmark: the auditor's verdicts rest on heuristic text parsing, and human labeling of a golden set is the pre-registered next step. It can only catch unfaithfulness that surfaces in the text: silent, never-verbalised reasoning is invisible to any transcript-level auditor by construction. Several runs are small (free-tier rate limits), and every follow rate is reported next to its sample size and minimum detectable effect. The cross-run ρ* agreement (0.708 to 0.800) came from a specification we have since shown to report a substantial mediated effect on a null, so it is neither test-retest reliability nor a current measurement. It is also invariant to the direct-effect path by construction, so agreement across runs would not have been evidence about hidden-path strength even under a correct fit. The full limitations list, the pre-registration, and every transcript are public in the repo.

06 · why uncertainty matters

The same numbers, two different conclusions.

A worked example of the failure mode this project prevents. The point-estimate version of faithfulness measurement would have us conclude that Model B is more faithful and deploy it for high-stakes oversight. The Bayesian version says we do not yet have evidence to support that conclusion.

Model B is more faithful by 9 points. Difference reported without an error bar.

Bayesian credible intervals

Model A
62 % [45, 78]
Model B
71 % [55, 84]
conclusion

Overlapping intervals do not settle a comparison. What settles it is the interval for the difference B minus A and the posterior probability that it exceeds a margin worth acting on. Both are printed here; read them, not the overlap.

Decision · Collect more data before any deployment claim

This is not a hypothetical concern. Every public faithfulness number in Lanham et al. (2023), Turpin et al. (2023), and the recent Anthropic CoT-monitorability line is reported as a point estimate without a credible interval on the faithfulness statistic itself. Importing 25 years of well-validated Bayesian mediation analysis closes that gap with very little methodological risk.

08 · phase 2, measured

What changed since the public version, and what the sweep found so far.

Everything below happened on 2026-09-07. An outside review found real defects in the estimator; they are now repaired and gated by tests. A synthetic battery of eleven known-truth mechanisms was run against the repair, the serving layer turned out to need its own determinism check, and the first powered real-model sweep is running. Every number below names its source file, and where a number is not yet measured, this page says so by name instead of leaving a blank.

Three estimator defects, found and repaired

No baseline intercepts. The pre-repair fit forced the mediator's clean-arm mean to zero and the outcome's clean-arm log-odds to Phi(0) = 0.5. On a synthetic null with a zero true effect (a Poisson-count mediator independent of treatment, an outcome coin independent of both, seed 731, n = 10,000), the old fit invented an NIE of +0.2131 and a TE of +0.2697 where the truth is zero. A Gaussian-offset version of the same null shows the same shape: old NIE +0.2213. The repaired fit adds mediator and outcome intercepts and reports NIE -0.0002 and TE -0.0082 on the Poisson null, which tracks the randomized arm difference (-0.0082) instead of inventing one.

A link mismatch between the two estimator paths. The PyMC posterior fit a logistic outcome; the maximum-likelihood fit, the closed-form integrator, and the pre-registered estimand are all probit. On the mechanism battery's own parameters, the same fitted coefficients converted under the two links gave NIE +0.2827 under probit against about +0.24 under logit, a gap of roughly 0.04 on an effect whose pre-registered verdict threshold is 0.15. Both paths are now probit, with one written definition of the estimand that every call site shares.

Fixed-scale outcome priors. The outcome intercept and the mediator coefficient carried priors stated in absolute units, which put the implied intercept several prior standard deviations from its mean whenever the mediator's baseline was large, like a reasoning-step count averaging six. The posterior shrank the intercept and the coefficient together and the mediated effect came out low. Rescaling every mediator prior by the mediator's own standard deviation fixed it: in the mechanism battery's PyMC subset (20 datasets per family, n = 350), NIE interval coverage went from 7/20 to 20/20 in the family with a large mediator baseline, and from 12/20 to 19/20 in a second affected family.

Sources: ESTIMATOR-REPAIR-2026-09-07.md and ESTIMATOR-PRIORS-2026-09-07.md. None of the three repairs touches a frozen file; tests/test_frozen_guard.py passes at every step.

What that means for the ρ* values already on this page

The real-model ρ* values in the ledger below (0.708 to 0.800) were computed under the intercept-free specification, on raw CoT step counts. One of the four runs has stored transcripts and could be refit both ways: ρ* moves from 0.750, which exactly reproduces the published number, to 0.327, and the mediated path does not merely shrink, it changes sign (NIE at ρ = 0: +0.415 old, -0.137 repaired).

That recomputation rests on 44 rows (22 items × 2 arms) whose control arm has no outcome variation by construction (every clean-correct item scores Y = 1), so -0.137 is reported as evidence that the published ρ* values are specification-dependent, not as a corrected estimate of anything. The other three runs have no stored transcripts and cannot be recomputed without rerunning the model calls, so those cells read "not available under the repaired estimator" rather than keep the old number quietly. Recomputation and figure regeneration are tracked as the next step in the same source document.

Averaged over 100 datasets each at n = 3,600, the two reports are the same report: both put nearly the whole total effect on the mediated path, both fire the pre-registered verdict in nearly every dataset, and their ρ*_point values differ by 0.0006 against a mean interval half-width of 0.0253, so the sensitivity summary separates a world with no mediation from a fully mediated one by nothing at all. A large ρ* is not a trust score, and ranking cells by ρ* alone would put a world with no mediation above one that is entirely mediated. What separates them is not a quantity this instrument produces from observational text: it needs an intervention, which is what the four-cell replay anchor (element 21) supplies.

serving determinism · measured on one A100 MIG slice

Batching breaks greedy decoding

Temperature 0 and a fixed seed do not make a vLLM server reproducible once more than one request shares a batch. Qwen3-8B, 30 ARC items, 320-token budget: at concurrency 1 a rerun matches the sequential run exactly; at 8 requests in flight and above, only 11 to 13 of 30 completions still match, and forced letter logprobs move by up to 0.875 nats. Throughput rises about 17× at 32 in flight, so the batching that makes a powered sweep affordable is exactly what breaks its determinism.

VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1 restores exact reproducibility at every concurrency level measured, at about half the batched throughput (a ratio of 0.40 to 0.49 against the flag off across concurrency 1 to 64). One caveat the throughput number does not show: the flag changes which greedy token wins; it does more than stabilize it. Against the flag-off sequential baseline, the flag-on mode matches on only 10 of 30 completions (median difference 0.125 nats), so the serving mode is now part of a model's identity and is pinned like a revision. Every powered cell now runs a determinism preflight on its own server before its arms start (30 items at 1 and 32 in flight, both must give 30/30 identical completions and a max logprob difference of exactly 0.0, or the cell refuses at exit 10); the first powered cell, Qwen3-8B on ARC stated-hint at n = 1,500, passed at 30/30 and 0.0.

10 · the obvious objections

Questions a careful reviewer should ask.

Why not just compute the correlation between CoT and answers?

Because correlation cannot separate "the reasoning drives the answer" from "the reasoning and the answer are both driven by the same hidden computation." Those two worlds produce identical correlations and opposite safety conclusions. Mediation analysis with interventions on the CoT is the minimal machinery that tells them apart, and the ρ sweep says how much hidden co-driving the verdict can absorb before it flips.

Why not just patch activations, like mech interp does?

Activation patching answers a different question (which internal components matter) and needs white-box access, which frontier deployments do not give overseers. This framework works at the level oversight actually happens: the text. The two are complementary; the methodology document maps the formal connection to causal scrubbing and interchange interventions, and the estimator applies unchanged if the mediator is a feature activation instead of a CoT segment.

Isn't ρ unidentifiable? You can't estimate what you can't observe.

Correct, and the framework never claims to estimate it. ρ is a sensitivity parameter, the same move epidemiology uses for unmeasured confounding (Imai, Keele & Tingley 2010; VanderWeele's E-value). The deliverable is a statement of the form "the faithfulness verdict survives every ρ up to ρ*", which is honest about exactly the assumption everyone else makes silently.

Why Bayesian instead of bootstrapped confidence intervals?

Three reasons. Hierarchical pooling: sparse prompts borrow strength instead of overfitting, which matters when some prompts have five usable traces. Small-sample honesty: posteriors stay calibrated where bootstrap intervals collapse. And decision-relevance: a posterior over the faithful path composes directly with a deployment loss function, which is how a safety team would actually use the number.

What would falsify this?

The pre-registered next study froze its pass / fail before any frontier run: a planted wrong hint must be followed on ≥ 30 % of right-but-uncertain items, a majority of that following must be silent, a neutral-reword negative control must change ≤ 15 % of answers, and ρ* must be measurably lower where unfaithfulness is more common. Anything else is a documented design review, not a silent reinterpretation. And no firing rate gets published as a measurement until ≥ 50 transcripts are hand-labeled by two independent raters, with the headline claim bounded by the auditor-vs-human agreement.
